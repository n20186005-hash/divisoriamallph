## 3. The halal & veg angle
Binondo has a long Muslim-trader presence, so **halal-certified and Muslim-friendly eateries** exist along Ongpin and side streets — look for halal signage. For **vegetarians**, the easiest wins are **Buddhist temple vegetarian food** (near Seng Guan Temple) and meat-free Chinese dishes (vegetable lumpia, tofu, pancit with no meat).

## 4. What to look for (examples)
1. **Halal-signposted eateries on Ongpin / nearby mosques** — rice meals, noodles, grilled items; confirm the certificate.
2. **Seng Guan Temple-area vegetarian stalls** — simple Buddhist vegetarian plates, often early-lunch only.
3. **Meat-free Chinese dishes anywhere** — fresh lumpia, mapo tofu (ask for no meat), vegetable pancit.
4. **In-mall food court** — some stalls offer veggie rice meals; check ingredients.

## 5. Tips
1. **Look for the halal certificate on the wall** — don't assume; ask.
2. **For vegetarian, specify "no meat, no shrimp paste"** — Filipino/Chinese cooking often hides anchovy or oyster sauce.
3. **Eat temple vegetarian early** — it's made in limited batches and disappears by mid-afternoon.

## 6. FAQ
- **Q: Is there halal food near Divisoria?** A: Yes, Binondo has Muslim-friendly eateries along Ongpin and near mosques — confirm the halal certificate on site.
- **Q: Where can vegetarians eat?** A: Temple-vegetarian stalls near Seng Guan and meat-free Chinese dishes; always confirm no fish/shrimp sauce.
- **Q: How far from the mall?** A: The Binondo halal/vegetarian pocket is roughly 8–12 minutes on foot.
