## 3. The pasalubong strategy
Pasalubong (take-home gifts) is the point of a Binondo trip. The classics are **hopia** (flakey bean/ube pastries), **ube/pandan treats**, and **kakanin** (rice cakes). Buy these **at the end** of your route so they don't get squashed in your shopping totes.

## 4. Where to buy (examples)
1. **Eng Bee Tin (Ongpin)** — the signature ube hopia and modern flavours; gift-boxed.
2. **Salazar Bakery** — old-guard hopia de custard / hopia baboy, no-frills and beloved.
3. **Ho Land** — another Binondo hopia name with boxed assortments.
4. **Kakanin stalls** — bilao of kutsinta, sapin-sapin, bibingka for bigger groups.

## 5. Packing & shelf-life tips
1. **Carry a separate rigid tote** for boxes — never stack wholesale bags on top.
2. **Ask shelf life at the counter** — hopia varies; some keep days, fresh kakanin is same-day.
3. **Buy fragile ube treats last, near your ride** — they bruise easily in a crowded market.

## 6. FAQ
- **Q: How far is the hopia belt from Divisoria Mall?** A: The Ongpin stretch is about a 5–10 minute walk from the mall's Tabora St side.
- **Q: Which hopia is the famous one?** A: Eng Bee Tin's ube hopia is the modern signature; Salazar is the traditionalist favourite — verify which branch is open.
- **Q: How long do they keep?** A: Varies by type; ask at the counter — some hopia keep several days, fresh kakanin is best same-day.
