## 3. The heritage-dining angle
Binondo is the world's oldest Chinatown, and its restaurants are part of the experience — not just fuel. The draw is **dim sum**, **pancit** (noodle dishes tied to birthdays and celebrations), and **roast meats**. These are the "reward meal" after a heavy shopping morning.

## 4. Classic houses (examples)
1. **Wai Ying / Wai Ying Fast Food** — Cantonese roast meats, pancit, congee; busy, share-a-table culture.
2. **Dong Bei Dumpling** — handmade northern-style dumplings; perennial queue.
3. **President Grand Palace / President Tea House** — dim sum institution on Ongpin; bigger, louder, festive.
4. **Sincerity (Cooks/Restaurant)** — known for crab and family-style Chinese plates.
5. **New Po-Heng Lumpia House** — fresh lumpia rolled to order (lighter counterpoint to fried fare).

## 5. Practical tips
1. **Go early or off-peak** — dim sum quality drops and queues grow after lunch rush.
2. **Order to share** — Chinese dining is communal; 3–4 dishes for two keeps cost and waste down.
3. **Cash + small bills** — many old houses are cash-first; splitting the bill is easier with change.

## 6. FAQ
- **Q: Which is closest to Divisoria Mall?** A: The Ongpin/Carvajal cluster is roughly 8–12 minutes on foot from the mall's Tabora St frontage.
- **Q: Do I need a reservation?** A: Generally no — walk-in only; expect waits at famous houses on weekends.
- **Q: What's a fair budget?** A: Ballpark ₱150–₱400 per person for a shared meal; confirm per house.
