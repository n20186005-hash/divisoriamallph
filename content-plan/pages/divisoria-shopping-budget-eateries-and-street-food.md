## 3. The budget-eats strategy
Divisoria rewards shoppers who fuel cheaply. Around the mall you'll find **carinderias** (home-style rice + ulam), **mami/goto/lugaw** stalls (noodle or rice-porridge soups), and **street snacks** (banana cue, camote cue, fish balls, kwek-kwek). Treat these as functional refuelling, not a food crawl — save the sit-down Binondo experience for `/best-food-near-divisoria-mall-binondo-food-crawl`.

## 4. What to look for (examples)
1. **Carinderia rows along Tabora / Tutuban side** — ₱50–₱90 plates (rice + 1–2 ulam). Fast, cash-only, no AC.
2. **Mami & lugaw stalls** — ₱40–₱70 hot soups; ideal when you're sweaty and tired.
3. **Street snack carts** — banana cue / camote cue ₱10–₱20; fish balls / kwek-kwek ₱10–₱30 with dipping sauce.
4. **Binondo budget canteens (5–10 min)** — if you want a proper Chinese noodle bowl without the queue at famous spots.

## 5. Hygiene & safety tips
1. **Eat where there's a turnover** — busy stalls = fresh stock and fast rotation.
2. **Carry small bills and alcohol wipes**; many counters have no card and limited change.
3. **Secure your bags while eating** — don't leave a tote on an empty chair; wear it across your front.

## 6. FAQ
- **Q: Can I eat inside Divisoria Mall?** A: The mall has a basic food area (see `/divisoria-mall-food-court`); for cheaper street options, step out to Tabora/Tutuban.
- **Q: Is street food safe?** A: Choose high-turnover stalls and freshly fried items; use your own wipes. This is general advice, not a guarantee.
- **Q: What's the cheapest filling meal?** A: A carinderia rice-ulam plate or a mami/lugaw bowl, roughly ₱40–₱90.
