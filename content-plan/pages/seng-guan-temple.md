## 3. Strategy
Seng Guan is one of Binondo's most prominent Buddhist temples — a serene counterpoint to the market. It's free, fragrant with incense, and surrounded by **vegetarian eateries** (see `/halal-and-vegetarian-near-divisoria`). A natural stop on a Chinatown walk.

## 4. What to see (examples)
1. **The main hall & incense offerings** — the sensory centerpiece.
2. **Upper floors** — check access; some areas are restricted.
3. **Vegetarian stalls nearby** — temple-adjacent meat-free food.

## 5. Tips
1. **Dress modestly** and speak softly.
2. **Don't photograph worshippers** up close without care.
3. **Try the vegetarian food** right outside if you're meat-free.

## 6. FAQ
- **Q: How far is Seng Guan from Divisoria Mall?** A: About 10–15 minutes on foot into Binondo.
- **Q: Is there a fee?** A: No — it's a working temple, free to visit.
- **Q: Is there vegetarian food nearby?** A: Yes — temple-area stalls serve Buddhist vegetarian meals, often early lunch only.
